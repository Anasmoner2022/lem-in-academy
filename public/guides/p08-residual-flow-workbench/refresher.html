CHAPTER 3 OF 14

Focused refresher

For an original arc u→v with capacity c and flow f, forward residual capacity is c-f and reverse residual capacity is f. Traversing a reverse arc cancels earlier flow; it does not represent an ant walking backward in the final colony.

Store a reverse index on every residual arc. Adding an original arc creates a forward residual arc and a paired zero-capacity reverse arc. If both u→v and v→u exist as original arcs, each gets its own pair; do not accidentally merge independent original capacities.

An augmenting path carries its smallest residual capacity, the bottleneck. Update both directions of every pair. At every nonterminal vertex, incoming original flow equals outgoing original flow. Integral starting capacities and integral bottlenecks preserve integral flows.

When no residual path reaches the sink, the source-reachable vertices form a cut whose original outgoing capacity equals the achieved flow. That certifies maximum flow. A target-capped run that stops early proves target feasibility but does not automatically yield a minimum cut.
